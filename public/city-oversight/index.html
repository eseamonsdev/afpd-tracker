City Official Remarks

These selected remarks were made by city officials during meetings and are presented from the meeting minutes. They focus on policing, police conduct, public-safety policy and spending, and the 2026 property-tax discussion:

  • American Fork City Council — Regular Session — August 25, 2026:

  • Ms. Montoya summarized that the interim budget included a proposed $660,000 property-tax increase to partially staff a new fire station. She reported that insurance-premium savings would allow the Council to reduce the proposed increase while maintaining the planned staffing. She noted that the Council could reduce the increase further, but any additional reduction would require corresponding changes to other budget items. View minutes, page 4

  • Council Member Taylor stated that he remained reluctant to support a property-tax increase but recognized that rising costs made it difficult for the City to continue providing essential services, particularly public safety, without additional revenue. He opposed using reserves for ongoing expenses and said he intended to support a reduced 5.7% increase. View minutes, page 4

  • Council Member John opposed using reserves to fund ongoing operations and supported the reduced tax-increase proposal. He later emphasized that supporting the increase was not an easy decision and said residents he had spoken with generally preferred smaller, more manageable increases instead of waiting several years and facing a substantial adjustment. View minutes, pages 4 and 6

  • Council Member Hunter said the proposal was consistent with comments residents made during the 2021 Truth in Taxation process favoring smaller, incremental adjustments instead of infrequent, substantial increases. He cautioned against treating annual increases as automatic and emphasized that each adjustment should still receive careful scrutiny and consideration of the burden placed on taxpayers. View minutes, pages 5–6

  • Council Member Carroll acknowledged that increasing taxes was difficult even when the need was clear. She explained that staffing the new fire station was a high priority because firefighter salaries were an ongoing operating expense that had to be permanently included in the budget. She said providing 24-hour public-safety coverage justified the additional cost and that she would support the reduced 5.7% increase. View minutes, page 5

  • Council Member Holley stated that he would vote against a property-tax increase because residents were already facing substantial increases in household expenses. He said he had proposed several alternatives and remained dissatisfied with how the purpose and proposed use of the increase had been communicated. Although he respected the other Council members' positions, he said he could not support the increase in good conscience. View minutes, page 6

  • Mayor Frost stated that the budget process, which began in February, would conclude that evening. He emphasized that the budget was one of the Council's most important responsibilities because it established priorities and funded services affecting residents. View minutes, page 7

  • American Fork City Council — Truth in Taxation Public Hearing — August 11, 2026:

  • Mayor Frost welcomed everyone to the public hearing. He explained the city was not adopting the certified tax rate that evening; the hearing was to allow the public to comment on the proposed property tax. The City Council would adopt the certified tax rate on August 25, 2026. Mayor Frost later asked citizens to be respectful and courteous, limited comments to two minutes per person, and asked that comments stay on the proposed property-tax rate. View minutes, pages 1–2

  • Ms. Montoya explained the purpose of the public hearing was to hear comments regarding the proposed tax increase. She referenced the Property Tax Impact Schedule and noted that the increase would partially staff the new fire station in the amount of $660,000. She also referenced the Notice of Property Tax Increase, which showed a proposed increase of $33.41 per year, or $2.78 per month, for the average residence. View minutes, page 2

  • Mr. Bunker explained that the proposal was for an 8.7% tax increase and that information presented by residents and business owners would help the Council better understand the issue. He said cities may consider tax increases because of inflation, growth, competition for employees, contractors and materials, or increased services. Mr. Bunker stated that public safety was critical for residents and explained that the budget included nine new firefighters for a third fire station. He said the station would cost about $1.5 million annually, with $660,000 coming from the proposed property tax and the remainder from the existing budget. View minutes, page 2

  • American Fork City Council — Regular Session — June 23, 2026:

  • Council Member Holley expressed concern that ongoing employee wage increases create long-term budget obligations that may necessitate future property tax increases. He stated that he would prefer to reevaluate some wage increases before delaying projects and reiterated that funding additional firefighter positions should be the City's highest budget priority. He also expressed concern that the interim budget tied the firefighter positions to approval of the proposed property tax increase and argued that the budget should communicate that public safety was the City's highest priority. View minutes, pages 8–10

  • Council Member Hunter emphasized that the interim budget included the firefighter positions and that the Council would determine final funding priorities during the Truth in Taxation process. He later stated that he believed there was sufficient Council support to fund the firefighter positions regardless of the final property-tax decision and that the budget could be amended if necessary. View minutes, pages 8 and 10

  • Council Member Carroll stated that the proposed firefighter positions represented a new service expansion and were therefore appropriately identified as part of the proposed property-tax increase. She also noted that employee compensation increases reflected rising benefit costs as well as wages and emphasized the City's commitment to maintaining competitive compensation. View minutes, page 8

  • Council Member Taylor stated that he shared the goal of funding the firefighter positions but did not believe the interim budget language created the same concern. He stated that he intended to support funding the positions in the final budget. View minutes, page 9

  • Mr. Bunker explained that revenue associated with the proposed property-tax increase would be placed in a separate fund and could not be spent until after the Truth in Taxation hearing and adoption of the final budget. He said the interim budget preserved the Council's flexibility and that the final budget could be revised after the hearing to reflect its priorities, including reallocating funds to support firefighter positions. View minutes, page 9

  • Ms. Montoya clarified that the Council could amend any portion of the budget, including during final adoption after the Truth in Taxation hearing. She explained that the Council could adopt any tax rate up to the proposed maximum and that staff would revise the final budget accordingly. View minutes, pages 8–9

  • Council Member John reaffirmed his support for ensuring that funding for public safety remained a priority while also emphasizing the importance of maintaining competitive wages to recruit and retain qualified City employees. He expressed confidence that the firefighter positions would be funded regardless of the final property-tax decision. View minutes, page 11

  • American Fork City Council — Regular Session — June 9, 2026:

  • American Fork City Council — Regular Session — August 26, 2025:

  • Police Chief Cameron Paul explained that the matter involved two investigations at Millet Massage. He described reports from patrons, traffic stops in which patrons allegedly admitted receiving sexual services, a search warrant served on July 10, 2025, interviews with workers, charges filed against two employees, and another investigation that remained ongoing. Chief Paul reviewed the city code governing massage businesses and asked the City Council to consider revoking the business license because charges had been filed and the alleged activity was still occurring inside the business. View minutes, pages 4–5

  • Ms. Schriever stated that the Council should consider the evidence submitted into the record and determine whether sufficient evidence had been presented to revoke the business license based on the Chapter 5 violations outlined by Chief Paul. She later explained that the Council could table the matter if it wanted additional evidence or move forward if it believed substantial evidence had been presented. View minutes, pages 7–8

  • Council Member Holley stated that the proceeding was not a criminal case but a business-license revocation. He cited multiple reports, employees staying at the business, and the departure of two employees. He stated that he found no reason to keep the business open for the safety and integrity of the community and disagreed with the argument that the allegations did not constitute evidence for the Council's administrative decision. View minutes, page 7

  • Council Member Taylor questioned whether the employees were licensed in Utah and stated that legitimate massage-therapy schools should provide training regarding illegal activity and professional decorum. He also discussed the need to establish the legitimacy of massage-therapy businesses. View minutes, page 7

  • Council Member Carroll stated that the Council's role was to determine whether there was evidence of conduct that violated city code. She said the statements made by the police chief, separate from what might happen in court, were evidence for her consideration of whether city code had been violated. View minutes, page 8

  • Council Member Hunter questioned whether moving the two workers to another state for employment resembled trafficking, referring to material submitted by the business concerning forced or suspected human trafficking. He also asked whether investigators had collected records that could connect cash payments to patrons' visits. View minutes, page 8
