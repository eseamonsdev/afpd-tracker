Kitchen, Bronson T.

Overview

Officer Bronson T. Kitchen started working for the American Fork Police Department on August 5, 2024.

Officer Bronson T. Kitchen began working for the American Fork Police Department on August 5, 2024. Before joining AFPD, he held public-sector positions with Salt Lake County, the State of Utah, and the Utah Transit Authority.

Employment

Public compensation records show Bronson T. Kitchen working in several public-sector positions before joining the American Fork Police Department. In 2018, the Utah Transit Authority reported him under the titles Bus Operator Trainee and Extra Board Operator, a relief-driver position used to cover routes as needed. State of Utah records list him as a POST-certified correctional officer in 2020 and 2021. Salt Lake County subsequently listed him as a Deputy II in 2023 and 2024. By 2025, American Fork City employed him as a Police Officer I.

He also is the school resource officer for American Fork Jr. High School.

Related Police Encounters

The records below concern Officer Bronson T. Kitchen’s involvement in encounters with Eric Roundy and Aaron Booker.

In the Eric Roundy encounter, Kitchen and Officer Jensen went to Roundy’s home after a woman alleged that Roundy and his wife had harassed her. Ring-camera footage shows the couple declining to answer questions and asking the officers to leave. Kitchen and Jensen continued knocking and ringing the doorbell for approximately 15 minutes and told the couple they needed to come to the door, although the officers did not have a warrant. They left after the couple requested assistance from the Utah County Sheriff’s Office. Disorderly-conduct and obstruction citations were later mailed to the couple.

On May 21, 2026, Kitchen stopped Aaron Booker on Interstate 15 near Centerville for allegedly driving improperly in an HOV lane. Dash-camera footage appeared to show that the restricted lane had already ended. When Booker lowered his window only partway, Kitchen threatened to have another agency break it. In a subsequently recorded apology, Kitchen acknowledged that he had misunderstood the lane, became unnecessarily agitated, and should not have made the threat. The department requested a formal complaint after the video became widely viewed, but a final internal-affairs finding has not been located publicly.
