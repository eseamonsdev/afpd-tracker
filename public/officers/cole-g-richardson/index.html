Chief Paul’s comments

  • The following is the complete discussion of Richardson’s March 8, 2026 traffic stop, including Daniel Sroka’s questions for context. The quotations are taken from the final edited transcript of Chief Cameron Paul’s September 16, 2026 interview with Daniel Sroka (pages 2–3).

  • Daniel Sroka: “Okay, let's go through the four incidents. Those are the incidents that I'm most familiar with. The first one is the police report for the stop-sign incident where Tyler Shaw was pulled over for the stop sign. Have you had a chance to review that footage in the video?”

  • Chief Cameron Paul: “Yes.”

  • Daniel Sroka: “What did you find?”

  • Chief Cameron Paul: “There are some issues. Officer Richardson—I understand that he thought he knew what the statute said, but he didn't. Looking specifically at the stop, I want to talk about reasonable suspicion and then also the stop-sign violation.”

  • Chief Cameron Paul: “It's hard to justify any type of stop-sign violation there. State code talks about stopping at a stop line or stop sign. It also talks about stopping at a point nearest the stop sign where you can still see traffic flowing back and forth, which you could argue also puts it a little past the stop sign.”

  • Daniel Sroka: “I do want to add a correction. In the Utah code, it does not say anywhere “nearest to the stop sign,” but it does say “before the intersection.” There are three places where someone needs to stop if there is a stop sign. Number one is at a well-marked stop line. If there is no well-marked stop line, then it is before the crosswalk. If there is no crosswalk, then it is before the intersection. The stop sign itself doesn't indicate the location where the stop needs to occur; the type of road does. Do we agree on that?”

  • Chief Cameron Paul: “Yes. I'm sorry, I don't have the exact language. What I'm saying is, the stop sign really doesn't matter. You're able to stop at a sign or at a place where you can see the intersection and clearly see traffic that's coming forward.”

  • Chief Cameron Paul: “In this case, I know he cited a stop-sign violation. I do not believe there was a stop-sign violation. We've since gone over the statute with him and said, “Officer Richardson, this is exactly what the statute says.” He didn't remember it correctly.”

  • Chief Cameron Paul: “I do not believe he was intentionally lying or fabricating, but he clearly misremembered, and it was clearly not a stop-sign violation.”

  • Chief Cameron Paul: “I would argue, however, that he did at least have reasonable suspicion to make the stop. He should have based his stop on that reasonable suspicion to begin with and not tried to drum up some kind of other offense.”

  • Daniel Sroka: “What was the reasonable suspicion that you believe he had?”

  • Chief Cameron Paul: “Again, I'm going to write this down because I've got my findings, but I don't have them in front of me. I'm not going to misspeak or be misquoted. I'm going to write that down as one of your questions: What established what I felt was reasonable suspicion for the stop? When we meet again, I'm going to have an answer for you. I just want to have my stuff in front of me.”
