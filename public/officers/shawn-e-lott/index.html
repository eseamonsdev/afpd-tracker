Lott, Shawn E.

Overview

Shawn E. Lott has worked for the American Fork Police Department since 2014 and was listed as a police sergeant in 2025. His career has included patrol, detective, and task-force assignments. He received the Utah Chiefs of Police Association’s Officer of the Year award in the midsize-agency category for his work during 2022.

This page also documents Lott’s involvement in the Joseph Ferreri arrest and the mistaken-identity arrest of Michael Anthony Roy. Both encounters later became the subjects of federal civil-rights lawsuits.

Employment

Shawn E. Lott has worked for the American Fork Police Department since 2014, following his graduation from the Salt Lake Community College Police Academy that April. His career has included patrol and detective assignments, and he was listed as a police sergeant in 2025.

In a search-warrant affidavit, Lott described his assignment as a detective with the Utah County Major Crimes Task Force, working on the SET team. His task-force work included narcotics investigations. In 2023, he received the Utah Chiefs of Police Association’s Officer of the Year award in the midsize-agency category for his work during 2022.

Alongside his police employment, Lott worked as a noncredit public-safety instructor at Utah Valley University from 2022 through 2025, with additional specialist entries in 2023 and 2024. He also serves on the board of the Utah Gang Investigators Association.

Related Police Encounters

The records below concern Lott’s involvement in the Joseph Ferreri arrest and the mistaken-identity arrest of Michael Anthony Roy.

In the Ferreri matter, Lott led the investigation and signed the probable-cause affidavit supporting Ferreri’s arrest. The affidavit stated that Ferreri drove his wife to and from massage businesses where she allegedly committed criminal acts. It also stated that Ferreri told Lott he suspected she might be doing something because she had previously been arrested for the same conduct. The charges against Ferreri were dismissed in December 2021. His federal civil-rights lawsuit was later settled and dismissed with prejudice in May 2025.

In the Roy matter, officers were seeking Michael Schwabland but instead detained Michael Anthony Roy. The department’s internal-affairs report states that Lott recognized Roy but failed to notify the other officers of the mistaken identity. Roy alleged that officers struck, kicked, and tased him during the encounter. His subsequent federal civil-rights lawsuit was resolved through a settlement.
