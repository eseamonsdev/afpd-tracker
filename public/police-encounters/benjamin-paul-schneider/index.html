Timeline

This timeline covers the American Fork incidents and later evidence collection. Police reports describe the officers’ accounts; differing explanations from participants are identified where relevant.

  • March 8, 2026 — Complaint, vehicle stop, and trespass notices

    • AFPD responded to a complaint about a package delivery and filming near a residence. The March 8 report records that Ben, Tyler Shaw, and videographer Benjamin Adams received trespass notices and were released without criminal charges.

    • Tonga, Spencer

    • Tonga interviewed Benjamin Adams, reviewed his camera roll with consent, and photographed relevant text messages. He then spoke with Ben and Tyler Shaw at the vehicle stop and issued trespass notices. His account appears on pages 5–7 of the incident report.

    • Richardson, Cole G.

    • Richardson stopped the vehicle driven by Tyler Shaw, with Ben as a passenger. His supplement says he observed stop-sign violations, took no action on those traffic offenses, and turned the investigation over to Tonga. See page 8.

    • Strong, Jaclyn G.

    • The department’s released camera inventory identifies Strong’s recording for this incident. The written incident report does not separately describe her individual actions.

    • Supporting Records

    • March 8 incident report
    • Released body-camera archive
  • March 9, 2026 — Vehicle stop, sobriety tests, and search

    • A complaint about Christian Morgan’s visit to the residence led to another stop involving Ben and Tyler Shaw. Hawkins’ report records a vehicle search that found nothing of note or substance.

    • Hawkins, Steven G.

    • Hawkins spoke with Christian Morgan and issued a trespass warning, then stopped the vehicle associated with the visit. He conducted sobriety tests on Tyler Shaw, requested a police-dog sniff, and searched the vehicle with Fraughton. The report records a .000 breath-test result and release from the scene. See pages 6–9.

    • Fraughton, Kolten D.

    • Fraughton assisted with identification and assessing the driver for impairment. He handled police dog Bane during the vehicle sniff and reported an alert, then helped Hawkins search the vehicle. See pages 6–8.

    • Bibeau, Ronald J.

    • Bibeau is listed as a responding officer on page 1 and is identified in the released camera inventory. The written report does not provide a separate narrative of his individual actions.

    • Mecham, Greg B.

    • Mecham is listed as a responding officer on page 1. A separate account of what he personally did has not been located, so no more specific action is assigned to him here.

    • Supporting Records

    • March 9 incident report
    • Released body-camera archive
  • March 10, 2026 — Ben’s arrest and transport

    • Ben was arrested on proposed charges of stalking and targeted residential picketing. The records distinguish the arresting officer from the officers who transported him to AFPD and then to jail.

    • Sumbot, Luke A.

    • Sumbot investigated the complaint and spoke with Ben about serving civil papers. He contacted the Marion County Circuit Court, which confirmed that the case was valid and filed but had no date set. He then arrested, handcuffed, and searched Ben, and the sign was booked into evidence. See pages 10–12.

    • Pitt, Kyle B.

    • Pitt spoke with the complainant and contacted a Sandy police officer about related incidents. After Sumbot arrested Ben, Pitt transported him to AFPD to wait for transport to jail. See pages 11–13.

    • Valadez, Mckay A.

    • Valadez transported Ben from AFPD to Utah County Jail and signed the March 10 probable-cause affidavit. The incident report and booking sheet identify Sumbot as the arresting officer. See page 12 of the report and page 2 of the affidavit.

    • Canizalez, Samuel

    • Canizalez is listed as a responding officer on page 1 and is identified in the released camera inventory. The report does not contain a separate narrative assigning him a specific arrest or transport action.

    • Somerville, J. Tayler

    • Somerville is listed as a responding officer on page 1 and has recordings in the released camera inventory. The report does not contain his own supplemental narrative, so his individual actions are not described more narrowly here.

    • Supporting Records

    • March 10 incident report
    • March 10 booking sheet
    • March 10 probable-cause affidavit
    • Released body-camera archive
  • March 11, 2026 — Afternoon sign and phone encounter

    • This encounter took place before the evening Airbnb operation. The reports describe the sign, the GoFundMe explanation, Sheldon Norcross’s phone seizure, and his arrest. The officers’ accounts and Norcross’s explanation differ about his handling of the phone.

    • Richardson, Cole G.

    • Richardson questioned the group about the sign and fundraiser, checked with neighboring property owners, and told Sheldon he intended to seize his phone. Richardson’s account says he and Adamson believed Sheldon was trying to destroy evidence. He grabbed Sheldon’s right arm, helped restrain and handcuff him, searched him, and transported him to AFPD. See pages 8–9 and 18.

    • Adamson, Quinn J.

    • Adamson contacted the property owner about the sign, removed it after she withdrew permission, and gave it to Richardson as evidence. He helped seize Sheldon’s phone and moved his arm into a cuffing position while Richardson applied the handcuffs. Adamson’s report records Sheldon saying he was trying to lock the phone; Nicosia’s interview records Sheldon saying he pressed the power button to black out the screen, not delete anything. See pages 14–16 and 18.

    • Neil, Anthony S.

    • Neil is listed among the responding officers on page 1 and is identified in the released camera inventory. The written report does not include a separate supplement explaining his individual actions during the afternoon encounter.

    • Supporting Records

    • March 11 incident report
    • GoFundMe page captured March 11
    • Released body-camera archive
  • March 11, 2026 — Search warrant and Airbnb operation

    • Richardson’s warrant affidavit was sworn at 5:11 p.m., and the warrant was signed at 5:37 p.m. Officers then served it at the Airbnb. Five people were taken to AFPD for questioning. The warrant return records that Ben was arrested and no items were seized under the warrant.

    • Richardson, Cole G.

    • Richardson requested the warrant and wrote the operation plan. His supplement identifies the entry order and assigns Adamson to the perimeter. During the operation, Richardson covered the doorway with his patrol rifle while Tonga called people outside and placed them in handcuffs. See page 11 and the warrant packet.

    • Laycock, Jake D.

    • Richardson’s plan assigned Laycock to lead the entry team. The report says Laycock led officers to the basement entrance. Tonga’s supplement records escorting Ben to Laycock’s police vehicle for transport to AFPD. See pages 11 and 17.

    • Tonga, Spencer

    • Tonga assisted with serving the warrant, calling people outside, and detaining them. His supplement records handcuffing and searching a man and escorting Ben to Laycock’s vehicle for transport to AFPD. See pages 11 and 17.

    • Gonzalez, David

    • Richardson’s operation plan places Gonzalez on the entry team after Tonga. His participation is also represented in the released camera files. The written report does not give him a separate supplemental narrative. See page 11.

    • Jensen, Joshua M.

    • Richardson’s plan places Jensen on the entry team after Gonzalez. The camera inventory also identifies his recording for this incident. The report does not provide his own account of individual arrests or searches. See page 11.

    • Lott, Shawn E.

    • Richardson’s plan places Sgt. Lott at the rear of the entry team. The released camera inventory also identifies his recording. The report does not contain a separate narrative of his individual actions. See page 11.

    • Adamson, Quinn J.

    • Adamson’s supplement says he surveyed the Airbnb location before the operation and later served the homeowner with a copy of the warrant. Richardson assigned him to the perimeter. His recorded calls and texts also show him updating Chief Paul and Sgt. Stowers about the operation and coordinating the detainees’ movement to AFPD. See pages 11 and 16 and the linked recording analysis.

    • Paul, Cameron J.

    • Adamson’s recorded communications identify Chief Paul as receiving an update about the warrant operation and the people in custody. Only Adamson’s side of the call is recorded; it does not establish that Paul ordered a particular arrest or use of force.

    • Stowers, Adam R.

    • Adamson’s recorded texts and call identify Sgt. Stowers as receiving updates about the operation and the plans for detainees at AFPD. The recording does not establish Stowers’ own instructions.

    • Supporting Records

    • March 11 incident report
    • Search warrant 3352981 and return
    • Adamson’s raid calls and texts (BamMap)
    • Released body-camera archive
  • March 11, 2026 — Interviews, release, and jail transport

    • After the afternoon encounter and Airbnb operation, the records describe interviews at AFPD. Nicosia’s report records that Sheldon, Steven, Sierra, and Tyler were released. Ben was taken to jail on a further stalking allegation.

    • Nicosia, Wyatt S.

    • Nicosia interviewed Sheldon about the phone encounter and fundraiser, then interviewed Ben and the other people brought from the Airbnb. His report records their explanations and the later release of the four others. He told Ben he was under arrest for another stalking charge. The March 11 probable-cause statement explicitly says Nicosia wrote it. See pages 18–30 of the report and page 2 of the statement.

    • Tonga, Spencer

    • Tonga handcuffed Ben after the interviews, transported him to Utah County Jail, and booked him there. The March 11 probable-cause statement identifies Tonga as the submitting officer. See pages 17 and 30 of the report and page 2 of the statement.

    • Richardson, Cole G.

    • The booking sheet lists Richardson as the arresting officer. The supplements separately describe Tonga’s later handcuffing, jail transport, and booking. These entries record different parts of the encounter and are kept distinct here.

    • March 11 incident report
    • March 11 probable-cause statement
    • March 11 booking sheet
  • May 26–27, 2026 — Follow-up collection of online videos

    • A later supplement in the March 10 case records a prosecutor’s request to collect online videos relevant to the investigation.

    • Sumbot, Luke A.

    • On May 26, Sumbot forwarded Nicosia a request from prosecutor Russell Farr to download relevant YouTube and Patreon material. He supplied the account links. See page 15 of the March 10 report.

    • Nicosia, Wyatt S.

    • On May 27, Nicosia downloaded two videos from Ben’s YouTube account and added them to the case files. His supplement does not say he downloaded Patreon material. See page 15.
