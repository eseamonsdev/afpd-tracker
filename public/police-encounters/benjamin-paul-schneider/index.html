Timeline

This timeline covers the American Fork incidents and later evidence collection. Police reports describe the officers’ accounts; differing explanations from participants are identified where relevant.

  • March 8, 2026 — Complaint, vehicle stop, and trespass notices

    • AFPD responded to a complaint about a package delivery and filming near a residence. The March 8 report records that Ben, Tyler Shaw, and videographer Benjamin Adams received trespass notices and were released without criminal charges.

    • Tonga, Spencer

    • Tonga interviewed Benjamin Adams, reviewed his camera roll with consent, and photographed relevant text messages. He then spoke with Ben and Tyler Shaw at the vehicle stop and issued trespass notices. His account appears on pages 5–7 of the incident report.

    • Richardson, Cole G.

    • Richardson stopped the vehicle driven by Tyler Shaw, with Ben as a passenger. His supplement says he observed stop-sign violations, took no action on those traffic offenses, and turned the investigation over to Tonga. See page 8.

    • Paul, Cameron James

    • According to a rough app transcript of a later interview, Chief Cameron Paul said Richardson had misremembered the stop-sign statute and that he did not believe a stop-sign violation occurred. Paul said the department reviewed the statute with Richardson. He maintained that another basis for the stop existed, but deferred explaining that basis until he could consult his findings.

    • Strong, Jaclyn Grace

    • The department’s released camera inventory identifies Strong’s recording for this incident. The written incident report does not separately describe her individual actions.

    • Evidence and discrepancies

    • The dashcam shows Shaw’s car pause before turning left, around 13:13:32–33, contrary to the report’s claim that he did not stop completely. The required stopping location is a separate question. John Bryan also questions why the occupants remained detained for Tonga after Richardson said he was giving a verbal warning.

  • March 9, 2026 — Vehicle stop, sobriety tests, and search

    • A complaint about Christian Morgan’s visit to the residence led to another stop involving Ben and Tyler Shaw. Hawkins’ report records a vehicle search that found nothing of note or substance.

    • Hawkins, Steven G.

    • Hawkins spoke with Christian Morgan and issued a trespass warning, then stopped the vehicle associated with the visit. He conducted sobriety tests on Tyler Shaw, requested a police-dog sniff, and searched the vehicle with Fraughton. The report records a .000 breath-test result and release from the scene. See pages 6–9.

    • Fraughton, Kolten D.

    • Fraughton assisted with identification and assessing the driver for impairment. He handled police dog Bane during the vehicle sniff and reported an alert, then helped Hawkins search the vehicle. See pages 6–8.

    • Bibeau, Ronald J.

    • Bibeau is listed as a responding officer on page 1 and is identified in the released camera inventory. The written report does not provide a separate narrative of his individual actions.

    • Mecham, Greg B.

    • Mecham is listed as a responding officer on page 1. A separate account of what he personally did has not been located, so no more specific action is assigned to him here.

    • Evidence and discrepancies

    • John Bryan’s review highlights officers discussing frightening the occupants and whether they had charges or were simply tired of the group being annoying. He also questions whether the dog sniff extended the stop. The report records a .000 breath test and a search that found nothing of note; an empty search alone does not prove the dog failed to alert.

    • Hawkins’ report acknowledges that his body-camera microphone was muted during part of the investigation, limiting the audio available to check his account.

  • March 10, 2026 — Ben’s arrest and transport

    • Ben was arrested on proposed charges of stalking and targeted residential picketing. The records distinguish the arresting officer from the officers who transported him to AFPD and then to jail.

    • Sumbot, Luke A.

    • Sumbot investigated the complaint and spoke with Ben about serving civil papers. He contacted the Marion County Circuit Court, which confirmed that the case was valid and filed but had no date set. He then arrested, handcuffed, and searched Ben, and the sign was booked into evidence. See pages 10–12.

    • Pitt, Kyle B.

    • Pitt spoke with the complainant, Joshua Johnson, and contacted a Sandy police officer about related incidents. After Sumbot arrested Ben, Pitt transported him to AFPD to wait for transport to jail. See pages 11–13.

    • Valadez, Mckay A.

  • March 11, 2026 — Afternoon sign and phone encounter

    • This encounter took place before the evening Airbnb operation. The reports describe the sign, the GoFundMe explanation, Sheldon Norcross’s phone seizure, and his arrest. The officers’ accounts and Norcross’s explanation differ about his handling of the phone.

    • Richardson, Cole G.

    • Richardson questioned the group about the sign and fundraiser, checked with neighboring property owners, and told Sheldon he intended to seize his phone. Richardson’s account says he and Adamson believed Sheldon was trying to destroy evidence. He grabbed Sheldon’s right arm, helped restrain and handcuff him, searched him, and transported him to AFPD. See pages 8–9 and 18.

    • Adamson, Quinn J.

    • Adamson contacted the property owner about the sign, removed it after she withdrew permission, and gave it to Richardson as evidence. He helped seize Sheldon’s phone and moved his arm into a cuffing position while Richardson applied the handcuffs. Adamson’s report records Sheldon saying he was trying to lock the phone; Nicosia’s interview records Sheldon saying he pressed the power button to black out the screen, not delete anything. See pages 14–16 and 18.

    • At the October 14, 2026 lawsuit press conference, plaintiffs’ attorney Solomon Radner alleged that Lieutenant Quinn Adamson cut down and seized the GoFundMe sign from a neighbor’s fence on March 11. Radner said the neighbor initially permitted the sign and alleged that police pressured her to withdraw that permission before removing it. This is the plaintiffs’ attorney’s allegation, not a court finding.

  • March 11, 2026 — Sheldon’s interview at AFPD

    • After Richardson transported Sheldon Norcross to AFPD, Detective Nicosia interviewed him about the phone encounter, the fundraiser, and his involvement with Ben. Nicosia’s supplement summarizes this recorded interview on pages 18–23; it is not a verbatim transcript.

    • Nicosia, Wyatt S.

    • Nicosia noted that Sheldon had declined to speak with the patrol officers. His supplement records an initial discussion, a break, and a further Miranda warning, after which Sheldon agreed to continue. Nicosia asked about the fundraiser, Ben’s involvement, and the phone. He brought the phone into the interview room and had Sheldon demonstrate how the lock button opened Apple Wallet.

    • Sheldon said he had pressed the power button to close Apple Wallet and lock the screen, not delete evidence. He said taking the lead on the filming was his own decision so Ben would avoid further trouble after the previous arrest. He described a plan to film for about an hour, promote the GoFundMe, and leave, with permission from the neighbor to hang the sign. These are Sheldon’s explanations as recorded in Nicosia’s report.

    • Richardson’s warrant affidavit was sworn at 5:11 p.m., and the warrant was signed at 5:37 p.m. Officers then served it at the Airbnb. Five people were taken to AFPD for questioning. The warrant return records that Ben was arrested and no items were seized under the warrant.

    • Adamson’s supplement says he surveyed the Airbnb location before the operation and later served the homeowner with a copy of the warrant. Richardson assigned him to the perimeter. His recorded calls and texts also show him updating Chief Paul and Sgt. Stowers about the operation and coordinating the detainees’ movement to AFPD. See pages 11 and 16 and the linked recording analysis.

    • Paul, Cameron James

    • Stowers, Adam R.

    • Adamson’s recorded texts and call identify Sgt. Stowers as receiving updates about the operation and the plans for detainees at AFPD. The recording does not establish Stowers’ own instructions.

    • Evidence and discrepancies

    • The warrant describes a search for allegedly stolen LEGO merchandise, while the operation plan describes apprehending Ben. No items were seized under the warrant. Attorney Brandon Grable questions the connection between the evidence sought and the operation conducted.

Lawsuit

Sheldon David Norcross, Tyler Matthew Shaw, Sierra Nicole Lauts, and Steven Allen filed a federal civil-rights lawsuit against American Fork City and 13 police officers on October 14, 2026. The lawsuit is brought on behalf of these four companions of Ben Schneider (Reckless Ben); Ben Schneider is not a plaintiff. The case is Norcross et al. v. City of American Fork et al., No. 2:26-cv-01008-RJS, in the U.S. District Court for the District of Utah. The complaint contains the plaintiffs’ allegations, not court findings.
