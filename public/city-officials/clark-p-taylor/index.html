City Official Remarks

The remarks below are presented as recorded in American Fork City Council meeting minutes.

  • American Fork City Council — Regular Session — August 25, 2026:

  • Council Member Taylor stated that he remained reluctant to support a property-tax increase but recognized that rising costs make it difficult for the City to continue providing essential services, particularly public safety, without additional revenue. He suggested that the city explore changes to its budgeting practices in the future to help address increasing costs. He recalled that, during the City’s 2021 Truth-in-Taxation process, the first since 2008—residents expressed a preference for smaller, more frequent adjustments rather than substantial increases after long intervals. He opposed using the City’s reserves to cover ongoing expenses and indicated that he favored reducing the proposed increase from 8.7% to 5.7%. He noted that the revised increase would cost the average resident less than $2 per month and stated that, although he was not comfortable with any increase, he intended to support the reduced proposal. Council Member Taylor explained that the proposal to use reserves likely originated from a suggestion he made after previously stating that he would not support a tax increase. He had asked whether the city could use reserves on a one-time basis to proceed with the fire station and then adjust future budgets to accommodate its ongoing costs. He clarified that it was a preliminary idea that he had raised without fully analyzing its long-term implications. View minutes, page 4 · View minutes, page 5 · View minutes, page 6

  • American Fork City Council — Regular Session — June 23, 2026:

  • Council Member Holley confirmed that if the Council ultimately does not approve the proposed property tax increase, the budget items discussed during the previous work session would be revised accordingly. Council Member Taylor stated that, although the interim budget includes the proposed expenditures, no funds associated with the proposed property tax increase would be spent until after the Truth in Taxation process is completed and the Council adopts the final budget before August 30. Council Member Holley stated that he would prefer to prioritize funding for the firefighter positions over other proposed expenditures if the property tax increase is not approved. Council Members Hunter and Taylor emphasized that the Council retains full authority to amend the final budget during the Truth in Taxation process and determine how available funds will ultimately be allocated. Council Member Taylor acknowledged Council Member Holley's concerns but stated that any changes to the budget language could be addressed during adoption of the final budget following the Truth in Taxation hearing. Ms. Montoya explained that the Council may adopt any tax rate up to the proposed maximum and that staff would revise the final budget, accordingly, making changes to the interim budget unnecessary. Council Member Taylor responded that he shared the goal of funding the firefighter positions but did not believe the interim budget language created the same concern. He stated that he intended to support funding the positions in the final budget and noted that Council Member Holley could offer a motion if he wished to revise the budget language.
