Agency call signs

The agency part of a spoken unit identifier tells you which department is calling. J is spoken as “Juliet.” For example, the 5J in 5J171 identifies American Fork; it does not identify the officer by itself.

Radio language

Short meanings for terms you may hear. Codes and abbreviations can vary by agency; use the surrounding conversation to interpret them.

10-9
Repeat the last message. “10-9, my radio cut out” is a request to hear the message again.
Copy / copied
Message received or understood. An acknowledgment does not necessarily identify the person being addressed.
Affirmative / negative
Yes / no. A short reply may need the preceding clip to make sense.
RP
Usually the reporting party: the person who reported the incident. It describes a role in the call, not necessarily a victim or suspect.
PD
Police department. “At the PD” commonly means at the police station.
Car-to-car
Officer-to-officer radio conversation. The archive on this site covers AFPD’s car-to-car talkgroup, 6001.

Names, nicknames, and radio IDs

Look up a name you hear or an ID shown beside a clip. Most listed IDs have named-response examples across multiple days in the selected spreadsheet. Ivan / Bird uses October 24 exchanges and the roster. Choates, Sam / Sammy, and T-Ball / G-Ball include owner listening observations added October 27, documented in their notes. Associations do not identify the speaker in every recording.

The numeric radio ID beside a clip identifies a transmitting radio. A spoken call sign identifies a unit or agency; a talkgroup identifies the conversation channel.

An officer may have used an ID in these exchanges without being the speaker in every recording under that ID. Attributing a particular statement to an officer still requires further confirmation.
